TASK 2 Rate Fencing Experiment: Eliminating Rate Dilution
▼
Strategic Experiment: Switch between Strategy A (Unfenced Flat Discount) and Strategy B (Fenced Rate Strategy) using the controls above. Notice how Strategy B protects the $300 rack rate for late-booking corporate guests while offering the discount only to price-sensitive leisure guests who book 14+ days in advance.
Rate Fence Classification Matrix:
Non-Physical Fences (Rules & Restrictions):
  • 14-day or 21-day advance booking requirement
  • 100% Non-refundable prepayment upon booking
  • Minimum Length of Stay (MLOS: 2-3 nights)
  • Closed user-group login (Loyalty Club Member only)
Physical Fences (Tangible Product Attributes):
  • Room view (Red Mountain pool view vs. parking lot)
  • Bed configuration & square footage (King Suite vs. Double Queen)
  • Floor level and quiet elevator proximity
  • Included premium amenities (club lounge access, breakfast)

TASK 4 Group RFP Displacement Challenge: Accept, Reject, or Counter?
▼
Executive Decision Case: The Utah Medical Association submits a Request for Proposal (RFP) for a 3-day conference requesting a block of 45 rooms per night at a discounted group rate of $165/night (compared to your current $300 transient rack rate). They guarantee $45 per room/night in banquet F&B catering (which has a 65% contribution margin). Your unconstrained transient forecast projects 80% transient occupancy (160 rooms) without the group. Since your total hotel capacity is 200 rooms, accepting the 45-room group would exceed capacity by 5 rooms, displacing 5 transient guests paying full $300 rack rate!
